Parkinson's Disease Physiotherapy: What Families in Kerala Should Know

Sandra Thomas (BPT, HCPC UK) Published: July 24, 2026 6 min read Neurological Rehabilitation

Parkinson's disease (PD) is a progressive neurological condition that affects movement, balance, and coordination. For families across Kerala, watching a loved one experience muscle stiffness, tremors, or "freezing of gait" can feel overwhelming. However, early and targeted neurological physiotherapy plays a vital role in preserving mobility and quality of life.

Common Movement Challenges in Parkinson's

Parkinson's primarily impacts motor function through four cardinal signs:

  • Bradykinesia: Slowness of voluntary movement.
  • Rigidity: Increased resistance and stiffness in limbs and trunk muscles.
  • Postural Instability: Impaired balance leading to an increased risk of falls.
  • Freezing of Gait (FOG): Brief, temporary inability to move feet forward when initiating a step or turning.

How Neurological Physiotherapy Helps

At Physio Dynamics in Panamaram, Wayanad, Chief Physiotherapist Sandra Thomas utilizes evidence-based neurorehabilitation protocols tailored to Parkinson's management:

  1. Auditory & Visual Cueing: Training patients to use rhythmic counts, metronomes, or visual floor markers to bypass impaired automatic motor pathways in the brain.
  2. Amplitude-Based Movement Training (LSVT BIG principles): Encouraging large-scale, high-effort movements to recalibrate motor output perception.
  3. Dynamic Balance & Fall Prevention: Challenging stability through stepping exercises, obstacle navigation, and balance board activities.
  4. Trunk Flexibility & Posture Correction: Stretching tight flexor muscles to combat stooped posture and maintain spinal extension.
