Lower Back Pain: Physiotherapy Exercises You Can Do at Home

Written by Dr. Sandra Thomas (PT)
29 June 2026
7 min read
Panamaram, Wayanad

Lower back pain is one of the most common reasons people visit a physiotherapist. Whether you spend long hours sitting at a desk, drive frequently, lift heavy objects, or simply have poor posture, lower back pain can affect your daily activities and quality of life.

The good news is that many cases of lower back pain improve with the right combination of physiotherapy, movement, and strengthening exercises.

In this guide, our physiotherapists at Physio Dynamics share safe and effective exercises that can help relieve pain, improve flexibility, and strengthen the muscles supporting your spine.

Important: These exercises are intended for general guidance only. If your pain is severe, follows an injury, or is associated with numbness, weakness, or bladder/bowel problems, seek medical attention immediately before attempting these exercises.

Common Causes of Lower Back Pain

Lower back pain can occur for many reasons. Some of the most common causes include:

Poor sitting posture
Long hours of desk work
Muscle strain
Weak core muscles
Herniated (slip) disc
Sciatica
Degenerative disc disease
Osteoarthritis
Sports injuries
Heavy lifting with incorrect technique
Obesity and lack of physical activity

A professional physiotherapy assessment helps identify the exact cause and ensures the correct treatment plan.

Benefits of Physiotherapy Exercises

Regular therapeutic exercises can help:

Reduce lower back pain
Improve spinal mobility
Strengthen core muscles
Improve posture
Reduce muscle stiffness
Prevent recurring episodes
Improve balance and flexibility
Support long-term spinal health

Consistency is far more important than intensity. Gentle daily exercises often provide better results than occasional strenuous workouts.

5 Effective Exercises for Lower Back Pain recommended by Physio Dynamics, Panamaram, Wayanad

5 Best Physiotherapy Exercises for Lower Back Pain

1

Pelvic Tilt

One of the safest beginner exercises for activating your deep abdominal muscles and reducing lower back stiffness.

How to Perform

  1. Lie on your back with both knees bent.
  2. Keep your feet flat on the floor.
  3. Tighten your abdominal muscles.
  4. Flatten your lower back gently against the floor.
  5. Hold for 5 seconds.
  6. Relax slowly.
10–15 repetitions  |  2–3 sets
2

Cat-Cow Stretch

Improves flexibility of the spine and relieves stiffness after prolonged sitting.

How to Perform

  1. Start on your hands and knees.
  2. Slowly arch your back upward like a cat.
  3. Hold for 2 seconds.
  4. Then slowly drop your stomach while lifting your head and hips.
  5. Move comfortably without forcing the stretch.
10 repetitions
3

Knee-to-Chest Stretch

Helps stretch the lower back muscles and reduces tightness.

How to Perform

  1. Lie flat on your back.
  2. Pull one knee gently toward your chest.
  3. Hold for 20–30 seconds.
  4. Return slowly.
  5. Repeat with the opposite leg.
Hold 20–30 seconds  |  Repeat 3 times each side
4

Bridge Exercise

Strengthens the glutes, lower back, and core muscles that support the spine.

How to Perform

  1. Lie on your back with knees bent.
  2. Keep feet hip-width apart.
  3. Tighten your abdominal muscles.
  4. Lift your hips until your body forms a straight line.
  5. Hold for 5 seconds.
  6. Lower slowly.
10–15 repetitions  |  2–3 sets
5

Bird Dog Exercise

Excellent for improving spinal stability and balance.

How to Perform

  1. Begin on your hands and knees.
  2. Extend your right arm forward.
  3. Extend your left leg backward.
  4. Keep your spine neutral.
  5. Hold for 5 seconds.
  6. Return and switch sides.
10 repetitions each side

Daily Tips to Prevent Lower Back Pain

Small lifestyle changes can make a big difference.

  • Sit with proper back support.
  • Avoid sitting continuously for more than 30–45 minutes.
  • Stand up and stretch regularly.
  • Lift heavy objects using your legs, not your back.
  • Maintain a healthy body weight.
  • Sleep on a supportive mattress.
  • Stay physically active.
  • Strengthen your core muscles regularly.

When Should You See a Physiotherapist?

Home exercises are helpful for many people, but professional assessment is recommended if:

Seek Physiotherapy Advice If:

  • Pain lasts longer than one week.
  • Pain is getting worse.
  • Pain travels down your leg (sciatica).
  • You experience numbness or tingling.
  • Walking becomes difficult.
  • Pain follows an accident or fall.
  • You have recurrent episodes of back pain.
  • Daily activities are becoming difficult.

Early physiotherapy often prevents chronic pain and reduces the need for medication or surgery.
