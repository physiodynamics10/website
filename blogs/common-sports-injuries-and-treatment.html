Common Sports Injuries and Their Treatment

Written by Dr. Sandra Thomas (PT)
23 July 2026
7 min read
Panamaram, Wayanad

From ankle sprains on the football pitch to shoulder pain in fast bowlers, sports injuries come in many forms. Knowing what you're dealing with — and how it's typically treated — can help you seek the right care early and avoid the issue becoming chronic.

Here's an overview of the most common sports injuries we see at Physio Dynamics, and how physiotherapy approaches each one.

1. Ankle Sprains

One of the most common injuries across almost every sport, an ankle sprain occurs when the ligaments supporting the ankle are stretched or torn, usually from rolling the foot inward.

Physiotherapy treatment: Early management focuses on reducing swelling, followed by restoring range of motion, balance training, and progressive strengthening to prevent recurring sprains.

2. Hamstring Strains

Common in runners and footballers, hamstring strains happen when the muscle fibres at the back of the thigh are overstretched, often during sprinting.

Physiotherapy treatment: Treatment involves gentle stretching, progressive strengthening, and a gradual return to running and sprinting, guided by pain and strength testing to avoid re-injury.

3. Knee Ligament Injuries (ACL/MCL)

Sudden changes of direction, jumping, and landing awkwardly can injure the knee ligaments, particularly the ACL and MCL — common in football, badminton, and basketball.

Physiotherapy treatment: Depending on severity, treatment ranges from conservative rehabilitation to post-surgical rehabilitation, focusing on restoring strength, stability, and confidence in the joint before returning to sport.

4. Rotator Cuff and Shoulder Injuries

Overhead and throwing sports — cricket bowling, badminton smashes, and throwing sports — place repetitive strain on the shoulder, which can lead to rotator cuff injuries or shoulder impingement.

Physiotherapy treatment: Treatment typically includes manual therapy, shoulder mobility work, and a structured strengthening programme targeting the rotator cuff and shoulder blade muscles.

5. Tennis Elbow (Lateral Epicondylitis)

Despite the name, tennis elbow affects players of badminton, cricket, and racquet sports too, caused by repetitive strain on the forearm tendons.

Physiotherapy treatment: Treatment focuses on reducing tendon irritation, correcting technique-related loading, and progressive strengthening of the forearm muscles.

6. Shin Splints

Common in runners, shin splints cause pain along the shinbone due to repetitive stress on the muscles and bone tissue, often linked to training load or footwear.

Physiotherapy treatment: Management includes activity modification, strengthening of the lower leg, and gait or training load adjustments to address the underlying cause.

7. Plantar Fasciitis

This causes heel pain from inflammation of the tissue along the bottom of the foot, common in runners and athletes who spend long hours on their feet.

Physiotherapy treatment: Treatment includes stretching, foot and calf strengthening, and manual therapy techniques to reduce tension in the plantar fascia.

8. Meniscus Tears

Twisting movements under load, common in football and badminton, can damage the meniscus — the cartilage that cushions the knee joint.

Physiotherapy treatment: Depending on the tear, treatment may involve conservative rehabilitation focusing on strength and stability, or post-surgical rehabilitation if surgery is required.

When to Seek Physiotherapy for a Sports Injury

Seek Physiotherapy If:

  • Pain persists beyond a few days
  • There is swelling, bruising, or visible deformity
  • The joint feels unstable or gives way
  • Pain limits your ability to train or compete
  • You've had a similar injury before

Frequently Asked Questions

Many sports injuries, particularly sprains, strains, and tendon issues, respond well to conservative physiotherapy treatment. Surgery is typically reserved for more severe ligament or structural injuries, and is decided in consultation with an orthopaedic specialist.

Recovery time varies significantly depending on the type and severity of the injury, ranging from a few weeks for mild strains to several months for ligament reconstructions. A physiotherapist can give a more accurate timeline after assessment.

General practice favours rest and gentle protection in the first 24-48 hours after an acute injury, but the right approach depends on the specific injury. A physiotherapist can advise on the most appropriate early management for your condition.
