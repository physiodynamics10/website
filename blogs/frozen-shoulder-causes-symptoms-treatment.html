Frozen Shoulder: Causes, Symptoms & Physiotherapy Treatment

Written by Sandra Thomas, BPT
13 July 2026
5 min read
Panamaram, Wayanad

Adhesive capsulitis, commonly known as frozen shoulder, is a painful condition that causes severe stiffness and restriction of movement in the shoulder joint. It affects around 2% to 5% of the population, mostly individuals aged between 40 and 60, and is particularly common in women and individuals with diabetes.

The good news is that with targeted physiotherapy and joint mobilization stretching exercises, full range of motion can be restored, preventing permanent stiffness.

Important: These exercises are intended for general guidance only. If your pain is severe, follows a traumatic injury, or you suspect a rotator cuff tear, seek professional medical assessment immediately.

Causes and Risk Factors

Frozen shoulder occurs when the strong connective tissue surrounding the shoulder joint (the joint capsule) becomes thick, stiff, and inflamed, leading to bands of scar tissue (adhesions). Key risk factors include:

Diabetes mellitus (affects up to 20% of diabetics)
Thyroid disorders (hypothyroidism or hyperthyroidism)
Post-surgical immobilization (after breast cancer surgery or fracture recovery)
Cardiovascular disease
Prolonged shoulder immobilization due to injury

The Three Stages of Adhesive Capsulitis

Frozen shoulder typically progresses through three distinct clinical phases, each with varying symptoms and treatment requirements:

Stage Duration Primary Symptoms Key Physiotherapy Focus
1. Freezing (Painful Stage) 6 – 9 Months Severe shoulder pain (worse at night), gradual onset of motion loss Pain relief (TENS, heat), gentle passive mobility exercises
2. Frozen (Adhesive Stage) 4 – 12 Months Pain may diminish, but shoulder becomes extremely stiff and restricted Active-assisted stretches, joint mobilization, capsular stretching
3. Thawing (Recovery Stage) 6 Months – 2 Years Gradual return of range of motion, minimal residual pain Progressive strengthening, rotator cuff training, functional restore

Best Physiotherapy Exercises for Frozen Shoulder

Here are some of the most effective, physiotherapist-recommended home exercises to maintain mobility in the joint:

1

Codman's Pendulum Exercise

Allows the humerus head to drop out of the socket slightly to relieve pressure and stiffness gently.

How to Perform

  1. Lean forward, supporting your non-injured arm on a table or chair.
  2. Let your injured arm hang vertically downward.
  3. Gently swing the arm in small circles (clockwise and counterclockwise).
  4. Keep your arm muscles relaxed, using the momentum of your torso.
Swing for 1–2 minutes  |  3 times daily
2

Towel Stretch (Internal Rotation)

Stretches the posterior joint capsule, which is key to restoring internal rotation movements.

How to Perform

  1. Hold a small towel behind your back with your unaffected arm at the top.
  2. Hold the other end of the towel with your affected arm at the bottom.
  3. Gently pull the towel upward with your top arm, stretching the bottom arm.
  4. Hold the stretch for 15–20 seconds, then relax.
Hold 20 seconds  |  10 repetitions
3

Finger Ladder / Wall Crawl

Slowly encourages active-assisted flexion of the shoulder joint.

How to Perform

  1. Stand facing a wall, about three-quarters of an arm's length away.
  2. Touch the wall at waist level with the fingers of your affected hand.
  3. Slowly walk your fingers up the wall as far as comfortable.
  4. Hold for 5 seconds, then crawl back down.
Repeat 10 times  |  2 sets
