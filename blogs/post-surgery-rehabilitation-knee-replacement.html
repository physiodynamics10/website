Post-Surgery Rehabilitation: Recovering After Knee Replacement

Written by Dr. Sandra Thomas (PT)
28 July 2026
6 min read
Panamaram, Wayanad

Total knee replacement can relieve years of joint pain — but the surgery itself is only half the journey. Physiotherapy rehabilitation is what determines how well the new joint moves, how much strength you regain, and how quickly you return to everyday activities.

Here's a step-by-step look at what physiotherapy after knee replacement typically involves, from the first day after surgery through to full recovery.

Why Physiotherapy Is Essential After Knee Replacement

Surgery replaces the damaged joint surfaces, but the surrounding muscles, tendons, and movement patterns still need to be retrained. Without structured physiotherapy, patients often experience stiffness, weakness, and a limited final range of motion — even though the implant itself is functioning well.

Stage 1: Days 1–7 (In-Hospital and Early Home Recovery)

The first week focuses on protecting the surgical site while gently restarting movement.

  • Gentle ankle pumps and knee bending exercises to reduce stiffness and swelling
  • Practising safe standing, walking with a frame or crutches, and transfers in and out of bed
  • Icing and elevation to manage swelling
  • Early focus on regaining basic knee bend and straightening

Stage 2: Weeks 2–6 (Building Range of Motion and Strength)

Once the initial healing has settled, rehabilitation becomes more active.

  • Progressive exercises to increase knee bending and straightening
  • Strengthening the thigh and hip muscles to support the new joint
  • Practising walking with less support, and eventually without a walking aid
  • Balance and proprioception exercises to restore stability

Stage 3: Weeks 6–12 (Restoring Function)

By this stage, most patients are walking independently and building toward normal daily activities.

  • Continued strengthening, focusing on stairs, sit-to-stand, and walking endurance
  • Gait retraining to normalise walking pattern
  • Gradual return to light daily activities and, where appropriate, low-impact exercise

Stage 4: 3–6 Months (Long-Term Strength and Activity)

Full recovery continues well beyond the initial 12 weeks, with many patients continuing to gain strength and confidence for several more months.

  • Advanced strengthening to support long-term joint health
  • Guidance on returning to activities such as walking longer distances, cycling, or swimming
  • Ongoing monitoring of knee range of motion and overall function

Typical Milestones to Expect

Timeframe Common Milestone
Day 1–3 Standing and taking first steps with assistance
Week 2 Walking short distances with a frame or crutches
Week 6 Walking without a walking aid, for many patients
Week 12 Managing stairs and most daily activities independently
6 months Continued strength gains and higher activity levels

Actual timelines vary based on age, overall health, and pre-surgery fitness — your physiotherapist will track your individual progress against these general milestones.

Common Challenges During Recovery

  • Stiffness or difficulty achieving full knee bend
  • Swelling that persists for several weeks
  • Muscle weakness, particularly in the thigh
  • Fear of putting full weight on the new joint

A structured physiotherapy programme is specifically designed to address each of these challenges as they arise.

Frequently Asked Questions

Physiotherapy typically begins within a day of surgery, starting with gentle movement and walking practice, and continues in a structured way for several months afterward.

Most patients regain the ability to walk without support and manage daily activities within 6–12 weeks, though continued strength and confidence gains can occur for up to a year after surgery.

Mild stiffness can persist for several months and often continues to improve gradually with consistent physiotherapy exercises. Significant or worsening stiffness should be discussed with your surgeon and physiotherapist.
