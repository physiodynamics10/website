Best Exercises for Knee Pain: A Physiotherapist's Guide

Written by Sandra Thomas, BPT
24 June 2026
6 min read
Panamaram, Wayanad

Knee pain is one of the most common musculoskeletal problems affecting people of all ages across Wayanad and Kerala. Whether caused by arthritis, sports injuries, muscle weakness, prolonged sitting, or overuse, knee pain can make simple activities such as walking, climbing stairs, or standing for long periods extremely difficult.

The good news is that physiotherapy and targeted exercises can help reduce pain, improve mobility, and strengthen the muscles supporting the knee joint — often without the need for surgery.

In this guide, our physiotherapists at Physio Dynamics share the most effective exercises that may help manage knee pain and improve knee function, recommended by our lead physiotherapist Sandra Thomas, BPT.

Important: These exercises are for general guidance only. Always consult a qualified physiotherapist before starting any exercise programme, particularly if you have an existing injury, recent surgery, or severe pain. A proper assessment is essential to identify the cause of your knee pain and create a personalised plan.

Common Causes of Knee Pain

Knee pain can occur due to a wide range of conditions. Understanding the cause is the first step toward effective treatment:

Osteoarthritis
Ligament injuries (ACL, MCL)
Meniscus injuries
Patellofemoral Pain Syndrome
Sports injuries
Muscle weakness
Excess body weight
Prolonged sitting or inactivity

A proper physiotherapy assessment at our clinic in Panamaram, Wayanad can identify the exact cause and help create a personalised treatment plan tailored to your specific needs.

Benefits of Exercise for Knee Pain

Regular therapeutic exercises, when performed correctly and consistently, can deliver significant benefits:

Strengthen muscles around the knee
Improve joint stability
Increase flexibility
Reduce stiffness
Improve balance and mobility
Support long-term recovery

5 Best Exercises for Knee Pain

1

Straight Leg Raise

This exercise strengthens the quadriceps muscles without placing excessive stress on the knee joint — making it ideal for people with significant knee pain or those early in their recovery.

How to Perform

  1. Lie flat on your back on a firm surface.
  2. Keep one knee bent with foot flat on the floor, and keep the other leg straight.
  3. Tighten the thigh muscle of the straight leg.
  4. Slowly raise the straight leg about 12 inches (30 cm) off the ground.
  5. Hold for 3–5 seconds, then lower slowly and controlled.
10–15 repetitions  |  2–3 sets
2

Quadriceps Sets

Quadriceps sets help improve muscle activation and knee stability. This is often one of the first exercises recommended after a knee injury or surgery because it activates the muscle without joint movement.

How to Perform

  1. Sit or lie with your leg extended straight on the floor.
  2. Place a small rolled towel under your knee for comfort if needed.
  3. Tighten the muscles on the front of your thigh (quadriceps).
  4. Press the back of your knee gently toward the floor.
  5. Hold for 5 seconds, then relax completely.
10–15 repetitions  |  2–3 sets
3

Hamstring Stretch

Tight hamstrings are a frequently overlooked contributor to knee pain. Stretching the hamstrings reduces tension across the back of the knee and improves overall lower limb flexibility.

How to Perform

  1. Sit on the floor with one leg extended straight out in front of you.
  2. Keep the other leg bent or extended, whichever is comfortable.
  3. Keeping your back straight, reach forward toward your toes.
  4. Stop when you feel a gentle stretch at the back of the thigh — do not bounce.
  5. Hold the stretch gently and breathe normally.
Hold 20–30 seconds  |  Repeat 3 times each leg
4

Wall Squats

Wall squats are a controlled, low-impact way to strengthen the quadriceps and gluteal muscles, both of which are critical for knee joint support and stability during daily activities.

How to Perform

  1. Stand with your back flat against a wall, feet shoulder-width apart.
  2. Place feet about 30–45 cm away from the wall.
  3. Slowly slide your back down the wall into a partial squat position.
  4. Keep knees aligned over your feet — do not let them cave inward.
  5. Hold the position for 5–10 seconds, then slowly return to standing.
10 repetitions  |  2 sets
5

Step-Ups

Step-ups are a functional exercise that closely mimics everyday activities like climbing stairs. They help build knee strength, balance, and functional mobility in a controlled way.

How to Perform

  1. Stand in front of a low step or stair (approximately 15–20 cm high).
  2. Step up with one foot, placing it fully on the step.
  3. Push through the heel and bring the other foot up to meet it.
  4. Step back down slowly and with control, leading with the same foot.
  5. Alternate leading legs or complete all reps on one side before switching.
10 repetitions per leg  |  2 sets

When Should You See a Physiotherapist?

While these exercises can be helpful for many people, professional assessment is important to ensure you are doing the right exercises for your specific condition.

Seek Physiotherapy Advice If:

  • Knee pain persists for more than a few days without improvement
  • Swelling develops around the knee
  • Walking or weight-bearing becomes difficult
  • You experience instability, giving way, or locking of the knee
  • Pain is affecting your sleep or daily activities
  • You have had a recent fall or injury to the knee

Early intervention typically leads to faster recovery and better long-term outcomes. Do not wait until the pain becomes severe before seeking professional help.
